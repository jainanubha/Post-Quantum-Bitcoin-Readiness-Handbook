# 9.10 Falcon and the emerging FN-DSA standard

Falcon is a lattice-based digital signature scheme selected by NIST in 2022. The proposed standard derived from Falcon is called FN-DSA. As of 29-10-2026, NIST's project page describes its standardization as ongoing. Consequently, this section describes the candidate construction rather than asserting final FIPS 206 encodings or requirements.

## Construction and mathematical intuition

Falcon belongs to the family of hash-and-sign schemes based on lattice trapdoors. At a high level, the public key defines a modular relation. The signer maps a message, with salt, to a target and uses secret trapdoor material to sample a short solution to the relation. The verifier checks that the solution satisfies the public relation and the required norm bound.

The NTRU lattice structure enables compact representation. The GPV-style sampling framework provides a way to produce signatures whose distribution does not reveal the private trapdoor when implemented correctly. Fast Fourier techniques support efficient sampling. Unlike the rejection-sampling construction in ML-DSA, Falcon's signing process involves a different sampling strategy and implementation challenges.

## Why investigate Falcon for Bitcoin?

Falcon's compact signatures relative to other selected PQ signature candidates make it an interesting subject for block-space and bandwidth research. But candidate compression modes and encodings must not be conflated with a future finalized FN-DSA standard. Signing complexity, numerical precision, Gaussian sampling, constant-time behavior and fault resistance also require scrutiny.

For consensus use, the verifier would need a precisely specified, deterministic accept-or-reject result for every input, including malformed encodings. Full nodes need not run the signing algorithm, but wallet implementations must still implement signing safely. These two engineering concerns should be evaluated independently.
