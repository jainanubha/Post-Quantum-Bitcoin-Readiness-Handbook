# 9.11 Emerging standards and ongoing standardization

The 2024 standards are the foundation of NIST's PQC portfolio, not its endpoint. New research, alternative assumptions and implementation experience can change the set of available primitives.

## Status snapshot (2-11-2026)

| Development | Status | Relevance to the handbook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FN-DSA (Falcon) | Standardization ongoing | Compact signatures |
| HQC | Selected for standardization in 2025 | Alternative code-based KEM |
| Additional digital signature schemes | NIST IR 8610, May 2026; nine candidates advance to round 3 | Alternative signatures and assumption diversity |
| SP 800-230 | Initial public draft, April 2026 | Limited-signature SLH-DSA parameters |
| SP 800-208 | Final recommendation | Stateful hash-based signatures |
| IR 8547 | Final, October 2026 | Transition methodology |

A selection for standardization is not a final standard. A draft is not equivalent to a final publication. A candidate progressing to another round is not an endorsement for production use.

## Hybrid mechanisms and cryptographic agility

Hybrid mechanisms combine components based on different cryptographic assumptions. A hybrid KEM and a hybrid signature construction address different security goals. Combining primitives does not automatically preserve the desired security property: the composition, binding of messages or transcripts, and failure conditions matter. For a spending policy, requiring both signatures has a different security and availability trade-off from allowing either signature.

Cryptographic agility is the ability to replace or update primitives without unnecessary disruption. In Bitcoin this is constrained by consensus compatibility, the immutability of existing spending commitments and the need for users to migrate UTXOs. NIST's transition guidance can help frame inventories, dependencies and planning, but its federal transition requirements do not determine Bitcoin's consensus decisions or migration deadlines.
