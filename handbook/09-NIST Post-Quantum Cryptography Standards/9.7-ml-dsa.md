# 9.7 FIPS 204: ML-DSA

ML-DSA is the standardized module-lattice-based signature scheme derived from CRYSTALS-Dilithium. It is designed to support public verification in the presence of known classical and quantum attacks. Its construction is related to Module-LWE and Module-SIS.

## Key generation

A seed is expanded into public matrix material and short secret vectors. A simplified algebraic relationship is

$$
t=As_1+s_2\quad\text{in }R_q.
$$

Here $A$ is a public matrix over a polynomial ring, while $s_1$ and $s_2$ are small secret vectors. The actual public key encodes a seed for reconstructing $A$ and a rounded component of $t$; the secret key retains additional information. The formula is an explanation of the underlying relationship, not the normative key encoding.

## Signing and verification

The scheme follows a Fiat–Shamir-with-aborts style of construction. At a high level, a signer samples a masking vector $y$, computes a commitment derived from $Ay$, hashes message-related material and the commitment to form a challenge $c$, and computes a response of the form

$$
z=y+cs_1.
$$

The signer checks bounds on intermediate values and may restart with a new mask. Rejection sampling helps prevent the response from revealing secret-dependent information. Rounding and compact hints allow a verifier to recover the relevant commitment information despite not having the full secret relation.

The verifier reconstructs the public matrix, validates encodings and bounds, recomputes the appropriate commitment information and checks that it yields the challenge contained in the signature. This outline omits domain separation, seed derivation, hint construction and the exact rejection conditions; those details must follow FIPS 204.

## Standardized parameter sets

| Parameter set | Category | Encoded public key (B) | Encoded private key (B) | Signature (B) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ML-DSA-44 | 2 | 1,312 | 2,560 | 2,420 |
| ML-DSA-65 | 3 | 1,952 | 4,032 | 3,309 |
| ML-DSA-87 | 5 | 2,592 | 4,896 | 4,627 |

Source: FIPS 204, section 4, tables 1–2. An expanded secret key is not the same as seed storage: a 32-byte generation seed can be used to reconstruct the key pair.

For perspective, a BIP340 Schnorr signature is 64 bytes. ML-DSA-44's encoded signature is about 37.8 times larger. This ratio does not compare equivalent security guarantees and is not a transaction weight or fee multiplier.

## Security and implementation

The relevant signature security goal is existential unforgeability under chosen-message attack (EUF-CMA). An adversary who can request signatures on messages of its choice should not be able to produce a valid signature on a new message. Implementations must also address side-channel leakage, faults, secret randomness and malformed inputs. FIPS 204 provides pure and prehash interfaces and supports deterministic or hedged signing; an application must define its use precisely rather than mixing interfaces.

## Relevance to Bitcoin

ML-DSA offers a standardized candidate for public transaction authorization, but it is not enabled by current Bitcoin signature opcodes. A future integration must define signature-message construction, commitment and revelation of public keys, encoding rules and resource accounting. Its larger keys and signatures are relevant to bandwidth, storage and block space. Existing Schnorr aggregation and threshold constructions cannot simply be transferred to ML-DSA. Any corresponding lattice-based protocols would need independent security and performance analysis.
