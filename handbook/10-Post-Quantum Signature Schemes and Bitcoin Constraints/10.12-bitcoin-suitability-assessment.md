# 10.12 Bitcoin suitability assessment and case studies

The evaluation framework in Section 10.3 is intended to support use-case-specific decisions, not to produce a universal ranking of post-quantum signatures. This section provides a preliminary qualitative application of that framework. A candidate is assessed as part of a defined spending mechanism, not as an isolated mathematical primitive.

## Preliminary candidate matrix

| Criterion | ML-DSA | SLH-DSA | Falcon | SHRINCS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Specification status | FIPS 204 final | FIPS 205 final | Candidate; FN-DSA standard in development | Research construction |
| Assumption family | Module lattices | Hash functions | NTRU lattices | Hash functions |
| Public-key size | Kilobyte-scale | Tens of bytes | Candidate parameter-dependent | Compact commitment; revision-dependent |
| Signature size | Kilobyte-scale | Several to tens of kilobytes | Sub-kilobyte or kilobyte-scale padded candidate formats | Normal and recovery sizes differ |
| Persistent signing state | Not required by primitive | Not required by primitive | Not required by primitive | Required for compact stateful path |
| Primary implementation issue to investigate | Encoding, sampling and verifier costs | Hash workload and signature overhead | Secure signing/sampling and exact verification rules | State allocation, backups and recovery |

| Bitcoin consensus availability | Not deployed | Not deployed | Not deployed | Research proposal, not deployed |

The table reflects the cited specifications and research status as of October 2026. No candidate has been declared suitable solely on the basis of the table. Quantitative latency, memory and adversarial resource bounds remain unknown in this comparison.

## Application-specific case studies

### Everyday payment wallet

A frequently used wallet values manageable fees, signing latency and simple backup. Evaluate the complete spending witness, including key revelation and policy proofs. Limited-use hash-based signing may require separate keys and careful state handling for transaction replacement. Compare with stateless signatures under the same expected signing workload.

### Long-term cold storage and recovery

A cold-storage output may remain unspent for a long time, so durable assumptions, reliable backups and a usable recovery path are central. A larger infrequently used signature may be acceptable at one fee rate but unaffordable during simultaneous network-wide migration. The assessment must model both normal and emergency spending paths.

### Distributed custody

A custodian may require threshold authorization, geographic separation and share refresh. Independent signatures can represent a threshold policy but may consume considerable space. A joint-signing construction may reduce on-chain data while increasing coordination complexity. Compare the actual protocol's malicious-participant and availability assumptions.

### High-frequency off-chain signing

Payment channels sign many alternative commitments. Evaluate signing-volume limits, latency, recovery and conditional-payment cryptography. A construction optimized for one or a few signatures cannot be adopted without accounting for this different workload.

### Full-node validation

Assess valid and malformed input cost, memory and worst-case block composition on low-powered hardware. A compact signature that requires expensive verification may be less attractive for this use case than a larger but easier-to-bound construction.

## Decision record

For each candidate and use case, document the threat model, proposed spending path, required consensus changes, normal and exceptional operation costs, security evidence and outstanding questions. A defensible conclusion may be conditional or `insufficient evidence`. See the [evaluation matrix](../../tables/pq-signature-evaluation-matrix.md) for a reusable starting point.
