# 10.9 Implications for advanced Bitcoin protocols

Bitcoin's cryptographic dependencies extend beyond the verification of individual on-chain signatures. Protocols may rely on elliptic-curve key agreement, linearity, public-key tweaking or proofs of knowledge. A post-quantum signature opcode would address only the authorization mechanism for outputs that use it.

## Dependency inventory

| Feature or protocol | Classical dependency | PQ research direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taproot | Schnorr key-path spending and elliptic-curve key tweaking | New commitment and authorization mechanisms; P2MR discussed in Chapter 12 |
| BIP32 HD wallets | Elliptic-curve public child derivation for non-hardened paths | Seed-based PQ derivation; equivalent watch-only discovery may need redesign |
| MuSig2 | Joint Schnorr signing and key aggregation | New multiparty protocols |
| Threshold custody | Group signing and share management | Scheme-specific threshold protocols and recovery models |
| Lightning | Repeated signing, funding and commitment spends, encrypted transport | Channel lifecycle, signing volumes and independent review of transport and payment mechanisms |
| PTLCs | Adaptor signatures and discrete-log-based point conditions | Alternative conditional-payment constructions |
| Silent Payments | Elliptic-curve shared-secret derivation | New private address-discovery and shared-secret mechanisms |
| DLCs | Adaptor signatures, oracle attestations | Alternative conditional authorization and oracle security |
| BIP322 message signing | Script-defined proof of authorization | Support for new output types and verification semantics |

The table identifies dependencies and research directions; it does not establish the feasibility of any proposed replacement. A scheme that verifies a digital signature may lack the algebraic properties needed for a different protocol.

## Case study: hierarchical wallet derivation

A BIP32 extended public key permits derivation of non-hardened public child keys without knowledge of the extended private key. A PQ scheme can often derive independent key-generation seeds from a master secret, but this does not by itself reproduce public-only derivation. A watch-only wallet might require precomputed public commitments or a new discovery protocol. Such a design must also address address reuse, key backup and compatibility with wallet descriptors.

## Case study: payment channels

A channel may require participants to sign many commitment transactions while only a small number reach the blockchain. A one-time signing scheme cannot safely reuse a key for distinct commitment messages merely because only one final spend is confirmed. The funding construction, commitment updates, dispute resolution, timelocks, payment conditions and transport security must be assessed separately. PTLCs are a distinct design direction from deployed hash-timelocked payment conditions.

See the [companion dependency inventory](../../tables/bitcoin-cryptographic-dependencies.md) for a reusable reference.

## Research method

For each protocol, inventory the cryptographic primitive, exact property required, exposure window and failure mode. Determine whether the dependency is on a consensus rule, wallet convention or off-chain protocol. Identify replacement candidates only after distinguishing basic authorization from algebraic composition.

## Discussion question

If an output uses a post-quantum signature but an off-chain protocol still relies on an elliptic-curve shared secret, what security guarantees remain quantum-vulnerable? Which guarantees are independent of on-chain authorization?
