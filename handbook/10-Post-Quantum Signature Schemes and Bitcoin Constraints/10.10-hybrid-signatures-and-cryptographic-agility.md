# 10.10 Hybrid signatures and cryptographic agility

Hybrid authorization combines multiple cryptographic mechanisms in a spending policy. Such constructions can diversify assumptions or provide operational flexibility during a transition. They do not become secure simply by including a post-quantum component: the logical conditions and binding of the complete policy are decisive.

## AND and OR authorization

Let $V_C(pk_C,m,\sigma_C)$ and $V_{PQ}(pk_{PQ},m,\sigma_{PQ})$ represent classical and post-quantum verification predicates for the intended authorization message. A conceptual AND policy requires

$$
V_C(pk_C,m,\sigma_C)\land V_{PQ}(pk_{PQ},m,\sigma_{PQ}).
$$

A conceptual OR policy accepts

$$
V_C(pk_C,m,\sigma_C)\lor V_{PQ}(pk_{PQ},m,\sigma_{PQ}).
$$

If the AND policy is correctly bound to the transaction, both keys are required, and no alternative spending path bypasses either check, forgery must satisfy both checks. The construction may remain resistant to forgery if one component becomes insecure and the other remains secure under the combined threat model. This is an intuition, not a proof of any particular composition. Losing access to either required signing key can make funds inaccessible.

An OR policy permits a spend whenever either check succeeds. A quantum-vulnerable classical branch that remains independently sufficient can therefore undermine the intended quantum resistance, even if another branch requires a PQ signature.

| Policy | Security and availability trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| Classical AND PQ | Diversified authorization; both keys and signatures required |
| Classical OR PQ | More recovery flexibility; weaker sufficient branch can authorize a spend |
| PQ AND PQ | Potential diversity across PQ assumptions; greater data and key-management costs |
| Timelocked fallback | Availability changes over time; analyze every branch at each height or time |
| Mixed threshold policy | Flexible custody; examine every sufficient subset of keys |

## Composition requirements

The proposal must define the signed message, domain separation, algorithm identifiers, exact keys or commitments and the accepted signature encodings. Authorization for one branch or protocol should not be transferable to another unintended context. Ambiguous encodings and optional checks may create downgrade paths. If a common transaction digest is used, each scheme's message interface and any additional context must still be specified.

An output with an AND script leaf but a quantum-vulnerable Taproot key path is not protected by that leaf alone. Evaluate the complete set of independently sufficient spending paths, not merely the most secure advertised branch.

## Cryptographic agility in Bitcoin

Software libraries can replace algorithms, but Bitcoin UTXOs commit to spending conditions that must be respected by consensus. Versioned witness programs, script versions or new key types can provide extension points; they do not automatically upgrade existing outputs. Wallets must discover compatible receiving addresses, maintain algorithm-specific backups and migrate coins through authorized spends where required. Migration rules and activation trade-offs are covered in later chapters.

## Research questions

Can a hybrid policy diversify security assumptions while keeping recovery practical? How do transaction replacement and signature aggregation affect message binding? What conditions would justify retiring a classical branch, and how would users who cannot migrate be affected?
