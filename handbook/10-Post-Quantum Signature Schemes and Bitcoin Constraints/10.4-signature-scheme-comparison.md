# 10.4 Comparative analysis of signature schemes

Chapter 09 explains the mathematical constructions and parameter sets of standardized algorithms. This section compares representative signatures as candidates for transaction authorization rather than treating every post-quantum primitive as interchangeable. ML-KEM is excluded because key encapsulation does not provide digital signatures.

## Candidate families

| Family | Examples | Distinctive research consideration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elliptic-curve (classical baseline) | ECDSA, Schnorr | Compact encodings and existing protocol support; quantum-vulnerable |
| Lattice-based | ML-DSA, Falcon/FN-DSA, HAWK | Data efficiency, sampling, verification and multiparty constructions |
| Stateless hash-based | SLH-DSA | Small public keys; large signatures; no persistent signing counter |
| Stateful hash-based | XMSS, LMS | Finite signing capacity; safe allocation of signing state |
| One-time hash-based | Lamport, WOTS+ | Strict one-time key usage; key commitments and signature overhead |
| Bitcoin-oriented hash-based | SHRINCS, SHRIMPS | Compact normal-case signatures under specific operational assumptions |
| Multivariate | MAYO, UOV | Compact signatures versus public-key size and cryptanalysis |
| Isogeny-based | SQIsign | Compact representations versus computational and implementation questions |
| Other constructions | FAEST, SDitH, MQOM | Alternative assumptions, proof-system costs and maturity |

These examples include research schemes and candidates at different stages. NIST IR 8610 reports the additional signature candidates advanced in May 2026; FIPS 206 remains in development at the time of this draft.

| Scheme / parameter set | NIST category | Public key (B) | Signature (B) | Specification |

| ML-DSA-44 | 2 | 1,312 | 2,420 | FIPS 204 |
| ML-DSA-65 | 3 | 1,952 | 3,309 | FIPS 204 |
| ML-DSA-87 | 5 | 2,592 | 4,627 | FIPS 204 |
| SLH-DSA-128s | 1 | 32 | 7,856 | FIPS 205 |
| SLH-DSA-192s | 3 | 48 | 16,224 | FIPS 205 |
| SLH-DSA-256s | 5 | 64 | 29,792 | FIPS 205 |
| Falcon-512 (padded candidate format) | 1 (candidate) | 897 | 666 | Falcon submission |
| Falcon-1024 (padded candidate format) | 5 (candidate) | 1,793 | 1,280 | Falcon submission |

SLH-DSA rows abbreviate the corresponding SHA2 and SHAKE variants, which share sizes; the `f` profiles are larger and optimized for signing speed. Falcon figures are candidate-format values, not final FN-DSA requirements. ECDSA and Schnorr values exclude transaction-specific sighash bytes. See the references for precise encodings.

For ML-DSA-44, the signature alone is $2420/64=37.8125$ times the length of BIP340 Schnorr. But a comparison of raw bytes is not a security-equivalence claim, a fee multiplier, or a comparison of complete spending witnesses. A key may be committed to and revealed later, while a script path may need additional proof data.

## Selecting comparison metrics

Compare all standardized parameter sets in a separate reference table (see [signature-size comparison](../../tables/signature-size-comparison.md)). For variable-length encodings distinguish average, typical and maximum sizes. For stateful schemes specify the tree height and number of signatures, and for recovery constructions distinguish normal and fallback signatures. Record the version and primary reference for each entry. Do not infer performance from size or aggregate the dimensions into a universal ranking without justified weighting.
