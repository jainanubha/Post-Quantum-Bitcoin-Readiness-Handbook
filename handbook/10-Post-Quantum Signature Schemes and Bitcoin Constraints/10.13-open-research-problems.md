# 10.13 Open research problems and conclusion

Post-quantum Bitcoin readiness is a system-level property. A secure signature scheme must be specified, implemented and integrated into spending conditions in a manner that preserves deterministic validation, reasonable resource consumption and reliable user authorization. Existing outputs and off-chain protocols require separate assessment.

## Research agenda

| Research question | Evidence required | Related sections |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What security targets are appropriate for long-lived UTXOs? | Concrete quantum attack models, exposure and multi-target analysis | 10.3 |
| Which compact signature candidates merit further study? | Current specifications, cryptanalysis and reproducible measurements | 10.4 |
| How can large signatures be handled safely by consensus? | Complete validation semantics, resource bounds and edge-case tests | 10.5–10.6 |
| Can limited-use signing be made safe for Bitcoin wallets? | Signing-volume model, crash and recovery analysis, device coordination | 10.7, 10.11 |
| Can practical PQ threshold signing preserve distributed custody? | Security proofs, communication costs and group-key interoperability | 10.8 |
| Can aggregation reduce the cost of authorizing many inputs? | Construction, message binding and worst-case verification analysis | 10.8 |
| Which Bitcoin protocols need new cryptographic constructions? | Dependency inventory and protocol-specific security models | 10.9 |
| Can hybrid authorization provide meaningful assumption diversity? | Composition proof, complete spending-path analysis and downgrade tests | 10.10 |
| What evidence establishes wallet and device readiness? | Interoperability, recovery, side-channel and fault-resistance evaluations | 10.11 |
| How should candidates be assessed for different workloads? | Explicit requirements and comparative evidence, including exceptional cases | 10.12 |

## Key takeaways

- NIST standardization and Bitcoin consensus suitability are distinct milestones.
- Public-key size, signature size, signing cost and verification cost are different metrics.
- State management, multiparty signing and algebraic composability may be as important as raw signature size.
- New signature semantics require a precisely specified spending construction and conservative resource accounting.
- Research comparisons should be explicit about candidate status, assumptions, missing measurements and the intended use case.
