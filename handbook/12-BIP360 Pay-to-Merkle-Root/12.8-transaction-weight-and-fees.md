# 12.9 Transaction weight and fee implications

## Accounting model

Chapter 11 uses BIP141 weight accounting, $W=4B+D$, where $B$ is stripped transaction size and $D$ is the additional witness serialization (including the marker and flag once per transaction). Virtual size is $v=\lceil W/4\rceil$. A target fee rate $f$ in sat/vB gives an illustrative fee $\lceil fv\rceil$ satoshis. Here we apply the same model to the specific draft P2MR witness format.

Assumptions: one native SegWit input, empty scriptSig, two outputs with 34-byte scripts, one 64-byte default-sighash Schnorr signature, a 34-byte `<key> OP_CHECKSIG` leaf, no annex and matching tree depth for script paths. These are size-accounting examples, not signed transactions or network measurements. The P2MR cases are hypothetical until activation; depth-zero P2MR is excluded because it does not enforce the leaf.

## Control-block accounting

For depth $d$, raw control lengths are $33+32d$ in P2TR and $1+32d$ in P2MR. A CompactSize length prefix occupies one byte below 253 and three bytes from 253 through 65,535. Define $p(x)$ as that prefix length over the range used here. The per-input witness sizes (excluding marker/flag) are

$$
D_{\mathrm{key}}=1+1+64=66,
$$

$$
D_{\mathrm{MR}}(d)=1+65+35+p(1+32d)+1+32d,
$$

$$
D_{\mathrm{TRscript}}(d)=1+65+35+p(33+32d)+33+32d.
$$

At depth 7, P2TR's control block is 257 bytes (three-byte prefix) while P2MR's is 225 bytes (one-byte prefix), producing a 34-byte saving. At other permitted positive depths both prefixes match and the saving is 32 bytes. At depth 8 and above both use three-byte prefixes. Thus the simple expression $103+32d$ for the P2MR witness needs two additional bytes starting at depth 8.

| Depth | P2TR raw control | P2MR raw control | P2TR witness | P2MR witness | Saving (bytes) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 65 | 33 | 167 | 135 | 32 |
| 2 | 97 | 65 | 199 | 167 | 32 |
| 4 | 161 | 129 | 263 | 231 | 32 |
| 7 | 257 | 225 | 361 | 327 | 34 |
| 8 | 289 | 257 | 393 | 361 | 32 |
| 128 | 4,129 | 4,097 | 4,233 | 4,201 | 32 |

## Complete transaction example

The stripped serialization totals $4+1+41+1+2(43)+4=137$ bytes: version, input count, input, output count, outputs and locktime. Therefore $4B=548$ WU. Add the transaction-level two witness bytes and the per-input witness size.

| Spend format | Per-input witness (B) | Weight (WU) | vsize (vB) | Fee at 10 sat/vB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P2TR key path | 66 | 616 | 154 | 1,540 sat |
| P2TR script path, depth 1 | 167 | 717 | 180 | 1,800 sat |
| P2MR, depth 1 | 135 | 685 | 172 | 1,720 sat |
| P2TR script path, depth 7 | 361 | 911 | 228 | 2,280 sat |
| P2MR, depth 7 | 327 | 877 | 220 | 2,200 sat |
| P2TR script path, depth 8 | 393 | 943 | 236 | 2,360 sat |
| P2MR, depth 8 | 361 | 911 | 228 | 2,280 sat |

P2MR depth one uses 135 witness bytes per input, versus 66 for Taproot's key path and 167 for a comparable depth-one Taproot script path. It adds 69 WU relative to the key-path baseline and saves 32 WU relative to the equivalent script path. At 10 sat/vB, the complete transaction's illustrative fee increases from 1,540 to 1,720 sat relative to the key path. It falls from 1,800 to 1,720 sat relative to the script path.

## Implications

For multiple matched inputs, a per-input weight difference scales with their number, while the marker and flag remain shared. Virtual size is rounded at transaction level, not independently for each input. For trees with unequal depth, sum each input's own witness size. Explicit sighash types, additional stack items, annexes, larger scripts or future signature encodings change the result.

The difference between P2MR and P2TR script-path witnesses is small compared with the potentially much larger overhead of post-quantum signatures from Chapter 11. Any concrete future PQ analysis must specify where keys and signatures are stored, how they are encoded and what validation rules permit them. This chapter models the reviewed BIP360 design with classical Schnorr leaves only.
