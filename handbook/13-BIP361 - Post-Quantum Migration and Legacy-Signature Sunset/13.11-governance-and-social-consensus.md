# 13.11 Governance and social consensus

## Consensus validity is not social agreement

A BIP number identifies a proposal in a publication process. It does not establish consensus adoption or community endorsement. BIP361 is an informational draft describing prospective consensus restrictions; turning that framework into rules requires additional specification, independent review, implementation and a deployment process. Miners can select transactions and produce blocks, but full nodes enforce their own consensus rules. Wallets and services need to coordinate so users can interact with the network. Neither miner signaling nor a wallet vendor announcement alone demonstrates that a proposed rule is accepted by the broader network.

## Competing objectives

The authors emphasize prevention of quantum-enabled transfers from exposed funds and the systemic effects of such transfers. Those favoring a schedule can argue that waiting until demonstrated compromise creates an unacceptable coordination problem. Others emphasize preservation of existing spending rights and the risk of excluding users whose legacy wallets cannot provide new forms of evidence. A restriction might prevent theft while also preventing a genuine holder from accessing funds.

| Value or objective | Question to evaluate |
| --- | --- |
| Ownership continuity | Can an honest holder still authorize a spend? |
| Theft prevention | Can a recovered key alone authorize a spend? |
| Predictability | Are rules and enforcement dates clearly communicated? |
| Decentralization | Can independently operated nodes verify the new rules? |
| Accessibility | Are old wallets, small holdings and constrained users accommodated? |
| Monetary confidence | What effects might vulnerable or unspendable funds have on trust? |
| Privacy | Does rescue expose wallet hierarchy or ownership relationships? |

These criteria can conflict. A framework that maximizes one does not automatically maximize all others. A technical proof of unforgeability cannot determine a socially acceptable balance between blocked legitimate access and allowed unauthorized transfers.

## Activation and risk communication

The proposed intervals are relative to an activation event, not an established date of quantum capability. A deployment plan would need readiness criteria, notice, consensus-boundary behavior, treatment of unconfirmed transactions and a process for reviewing evidence as quantum and cryptographic research changes. Fixed dates provide coordination but may be too early or too late under different capability trajectories. Adaptive triggers can respond to evidence but may be difficult to verify or agree upon.
