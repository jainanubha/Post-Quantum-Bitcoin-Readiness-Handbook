# 13.13 Limitations, open research problems and conclusion

## Current limitations

The pinned BIP361 draft provides a migration framework but does not define the prerequisite PQ signature mechanism, all Phase A output eligibility rules, a complete Phase B rescue verifier or precise deployment parameters. The actual recovery coverage of legacy outputs remains unknown. The economic scenarios in this chapter use explicit synthetic inputs rather than measurements of the present UTXO set.

## Research agenda

| Topic | Question | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authorization | Which PQ spending mechanisms meet Bitcoin constraints? | Stable specifications, cryptanalysis and resource analysis |
| Phase A | How can eligibility be defined for hidden policies? | Consensus predicate and compatibility analysis |
| Phase B | What proof predicate prevents quantum theft while preserving legitimate access? | Formal security model and reviewed specification |
| Recovery coverage | Which wallets possess an independent secret? | Dated inventory, wallet construction analysis and uncertainty |
| Short exposure | Can late migration be made safe under rapid key recovery? | Threat-model comparison of migration designs |
| Early outputs | What policies are suitable for outputs lacking known independent evidence? | Alternatives and governance analysis |
| Capacity | What migration workload can the network support? | UTXO distribution, templates and demand models |
| Privacy | What new information is disclosed by migration or rescue? | Protocol and metadata analysis |
| Activation | How are readiness and enforcement coordinated? | Deployment proposal and independent review |
| Governance | How should conflicting access and theft-prevention goals be evaluated? | Documented arguments and community deliberation |

## Key takeaways

- Output-level protection and network-wide migration are related but distinct problems.
- BIP360 alone does not introduce PQ signatures or retrofit legacy UTXOs.
- The reviewed BIP361 draft describes two phases and envisages rescue as part of stronger Phase B authorization; historical three-phase descriptions are not the basis of this chapter.
- Recovery requires a defensible knowledge asymmetry. An approach suitable for some HD wallets may exclude others.
- On-chain dormancy is not evidence that a coin is lost, and exposed value is not equivalent to stolen value.
- Block-space, fee pressure, wallet compatibility and governance are critical to feasibility.
