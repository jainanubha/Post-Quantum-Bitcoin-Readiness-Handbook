# 13.7 Quantum-safe rescue and knowledge asymmetry

## The recovery problem

A rescue mechanism should authenticate a legitimate legacy holder using information that an adversary who recovered the relevant elliptic-curve private scalar does not automatically obtain. The existence of such information varies across wallets. The pinned BIP361 describes this as an area for further research; it discusses hardened HD derivation and refers to proof and commitment-based approaches, but supplies no final proof system.

## BIP32 as a possible source of independent knowledge

An extended private key contains a private scalar and chain code. Under BIP32, a hardened child derivation uses HMAC-SHA512 keyed by the parent chain code, with input incorporating the parent private scalar and child index. In simplified notation, for hardened index $i$,

$$I=\operatorname{HMAC\!\!-\!SHA512}(c_{par},\ 0x00\parallel\operatorname{ser}_{256}(k_{par})\parallel\operatorname{ser}_{32}(i)),$$
$$k_i=(\operatorname{parse}_{256}(I_L)+k_{par})\bmod n.$$

The actual BIP32 procedure includes invalid-child cases and derives a new chain code from $I_R$. The distinction is that knowing a child scalar does not normally provide an efficient inversion of the hardened derivation to recover the complete parent secret. This is conditional on the secrecy and entropy of parent material and the security of the underlying derivation. If a relevant parent xpub is known, its chain code is known, and a sufficiently powerful CRQC may recover its private scalar. Hence the level in the hierarchy and disclosure history matter.

BIP39 mnemonics are one way of generating seed material but not a requirement of BIP32. Imported keys, early non-HD wallets, alternative derivation methods and missing backups cannot be assumed to satisfy a proof of a BIP32 derivation.

## Abstract proof relation

A research design could define a relation $R(x,w)$, with public statement $x$ containing an outpoint, a public commitment and a transaction authorization message, and private witness $w$ containing seed or ancestor key material and a derivation path. The statement must connect the derived key and any script or Taproot tweak to the actual old output. An appropriate proof system would demonstrate the relation without publishing the recovery secret.

This is only a specification sketch. A complete design must prove soundness against quantum adversaries, define encoding and verification costs, bind the proof to the authorized transaction and rule out replay or substitution. It must handle Taproot tweaks and multi-party ownership. Proof generation on constrained hardware and preserving the privacy of derivation metadata also require investigation.

## Prepared versus unprepared recovery

A November 2026 developer discussion proposes a useful taxonomy: prepared schemes depend on prior actions or commitments; unprepared schemes attempt recovery using information already held by owners. It separately distinguishes hidden but not intrinsically private information (for example an unrevealed public key) from secrets such as a seed.

| Candidate evidence | Limitation |
| --- | --- |
| Unrevealed public key behind a hash | Can be disclosed off-chain or exposed during spending |
| Hidden script | May be guessable or previously shared |
| HD seed or hardened ancestor | Not universal; disclosure and derivation compatibility matter |
| Prior commitment | Requires appropriate timing, authorization and persistence |
| Legacy private scalar alone | Not independent of quantum key recovery |

Zero-knowledge proofs and commit/reveal protocols are different mechanisms for exploiting a recovery secret. The former may avoid revealing it; the latter generally relies on temporal ordering and the security of the commitment. Neither technique by itself guarantees that the secret is unique to a legitimate holder.
