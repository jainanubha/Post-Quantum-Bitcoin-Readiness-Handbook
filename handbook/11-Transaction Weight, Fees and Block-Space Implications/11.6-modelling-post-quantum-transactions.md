# 11.6 Modelling post-quantum transactions

A post-quantum signature standard defines a cryptographic primitive and its encodings. It does not define how Bitcoin should commit to keys, represent signatures in a witness, verify them or charge for their computation. This section introduces a controlled accounting model to investigate the consequences of larger keys and signatures independently of a particular proposal.

## Reference construction M1: revealed public key and signature

Assume a hypothetical native witness spending format with an empty scriptSig, a compact 34-byte output script, and exactly two witness items per input: the raw signature of length $s$ and the encoded public key of length $p$. The previous output is assumed to commit to the appropriate authorization key; the binding mechanism and witness version are deliberately unspecified. We assume current SegWit weight accounting only for the purpose of comparison. The input counts and output counts are $n,m<253$.

$$
B=10+41n+43m,
$$

$$
D=2+n[1+c(s)+s+c(p)+p],
$$

$$
W=4B+D,\qquad v=\lceil W/4\rceil.
$$

No script, Merkle proof, sighash-mode byte, algorithm identifier or additional metadata is included in M1. Such fields may be necessary in a real proposal. M1 is a serialization estimate, **not a consensus-valid Bitcoin transaction format**. Ordinary tapscript stack items cannot exceed 520 bytes; changing a signature alone cannot make M1 usable today. Refer to Chapter 10 for the design constraints.

## Alternative models

- M2 (signature only): the authorized public key is available through the previous output or another specified binding mechanism; the witness contains only a signature. A direct-key output may be larger, so creation cost must also be modelled.
- M3 (script/proof): the witness includes the signature, public key, executed script and commitment proof, plus any other fields defined by the proposed construction. Count the vector length and each item's prefix; do not assume a Taproot proof format applies to PQ proposals.
- M4 (hybrid): independently encoded classical and PQ authorizations are both required; add all required keys, signatures and proofs. An OR policy that exposes only one path needs a different model and different security analysis.
- M5 (aggregation): model an aggregate signature, participant metadata and proofs only where a concrete aggregation scheme exists. No aggregation savings are assumed by default.

M2 through M5 are alternative accounting templates, not proposed consensus changes. Their sizes depend on actual protocol choices. Even a small signature may require a large public key or proof; even a small output commitment may impose a high later spending cost.

## Input data

Use encoded public-key and signature sizes from [Chapter 10's signature-size comparison](../../tables/signature-size-comparison.md). ML-DSA and SLH-DSA sizes are from final NIST standards; Falcon padded candidate encodings are included only as a research comparison and do not assert final FN-DSA sizes. Private keys and seeds are never part of the transaction model.

## Example: encoding overhead

For ML-DSA-44, $s=2420$, $p=1312$, and both lengths require 3-byte CompactSize prefixes. The two-item witness vector occupies $1+3+2420+3+1312=3739$ B. For SLH-DSA-128s, $s=7856$, $p=32$, giving $1+3+7856+1+32=7893$ B. The marker and flag are shared across the transaction, not repeated per input.

If the output script changes size, the generic formula from Section 11.2 replaces the 43-byte assumption. If more than 252 inputs or outputs are used, the count prefixes must also be recomputed.
